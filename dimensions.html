The five dimensions.

Discovery, Direction, Depth, Dignity and Delight. They are not a marketing framework. They are the design brief, and every decision about a 5D community is tested against them.

  • 1

    Discovery

    The first real season of asking what you actually want. Not what the household needed, not what the job required. Communities are built to make trying something new easy and unembarrassing, because curiosity is a muscle that comes back quickly once it is used.

    In practice: a studio for making things, shared transport to classes and trailheads, and a calendar that residents fill themselves.

  • 2

    Direction

    Purpose does not retire. Many women arrive with decades of expertise and nowhere obvious to put it. We build for contribution: mentoring, teaching, consulting, volunteering, building the thing you never had time to build.

    In practice: a work room with real desks and good internet, and a community that treats ambition at seventy as ordinary.

  • 3

    Depth

    Friendship at this stage is hard to manufacture and impossible to fake. Depth is what happens when a small number of people share meals, weather and ordinary days over years. It cannot be programmed, only made likely.

    In practice: small communities rather than large ones, shared dinners several nights a week, and homes that face one another across common ground.

  • 4

    Dignity

    Your own front door. Your own key. A home you own rather than a room you are assigned. Nobody checking on you, and nobody deciding for you. Safety and independence are not opposites, and we refuse to trade one for the other.

    In practice: private, deeded homes, step-free design from the start, secure lighting and entry, and no staff with authority over your day.

  • 5

    Delight

    The part most retirement housing forgets entirely. Good light, good food, a garden worth walking through, colour, music, a fire in the evening. Beauty is not decoration at this stage of life. It is the point.

    In practice: architecture with intent, a kitchen worth cooking in, landscape designed for shade and scent, and gathering spaces built for long evenings.

Why five, and why these five.

Conventional senior housing optimizes for one dimension, safety, and quietly sacrifices the other four to get it. The result is a place that is very good at preventing falls and very bad at being a life.

Holding five dimensions at once is harder and more expensive. It is also the entire reason this company exists.

  • They shape the site plan

    Homes face shared ground because Depth depends on ordinary daily contact, not scheduled events.

  • They shape the buildings

    Private entries and owned homes because Dignity cannot be retrofitted onto a dormitory.

  • They shape the budget

    Money goes into the common house and the landscape, where Discovery and Delight actually happen.

  • They shape who joins

    Residents choose one another, because Direction and Depth need people who want the same things.
